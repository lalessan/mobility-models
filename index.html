Five questions to ask of a model

Question One end The other end
What is it for? explain predict
How is it built? from a mechanism from data
How many parameters? none many
What does it describe? individuals aggregates
Where does it work? one place anywhere

Model 1: gravity

The number of people travelling from \(i\) to \(j\) grows with the size of both places and falls with the distance between them:

\[ T_{ij} = K \, \frac{m_i^{\alpha} \, m_j^{\beta}}{d_{ij}^{\gamma}} \qquad \text{or, with exponential decay,} \qquad T_{ij} = K \, m_i^{\alpha} \, m_j^{\beta} \, e^{-d_{ij}/d_0} \]

\(m_i\): workers living in \(i\) · \(m_j\): jobs in \(j\) · \(d_{ij}\): distance · \(K\): chosen so that the total predicted equals the total observed. Zipf (1946) used \(\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 1\).

How good is a model? We use the common part of commuters: the share of commuters the model sends to the right place (1 is perfect, 0 is nothing right).

\[ \text{CPC} = \frac{2 \sum_{ij} \min(T_{ij}, \hat{T}_{ij})}{\sum_{ij} T_{ij} + \sum_{ij} \hat{T}_{ij}} \]

\(T_{ij}\): real commuters from \(i\) to \(j\) · \(\hat{T}_{ij}\): the model’s prediction.

Exercise 1: fit the gravity model 20 min

  1. Predict (3 min). Before opening the playground, write down 2–3 pairs of municipalities where you expect the model to fail.
  2. Explore (10 min). Open the playground. Fit \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\gamma\). How well does the model do?
  3. Check (5 min). Were your predictions right? Where does it fail, and why? Write it in the shared doc.

Model 2: radiation

A job seeker in \(i\) gets offers everywhere, each with a random quality drawn the same way everywhere, and takes the closest offer that is better than the best one at home. Counting how often that offer is in \(j\) gives

\[ T_{ij} = T_i \, \frac{m_i \, n_j}{(m_i + s_{ij})(m_i + n_j + s_{ij})} \]

\(T_i\): commuters leaving \(i\) · \(m_i\): workers living in \(i\) · \(n_j\): jobs in \(j\) · \(s_{ij}\): jobs in all municipalities closer to \(i\) than \(j\) is. No parameters to fit. Simini, González, Maritan & Barabási (2012).

Exercise 2: gravity vs radiation 15 min

  1. Switch (3 min). Set the playground to Radiation. There is nothing to fit.
  2. Compare (7 min). CPC vs gravity (power law and exponential). Where does the orange line leave the diagonal?
  3. Explain (5 min). Rank the biggest misses by number of people. Where does radiation send too many people, and where too few? Why?

And then: which model would you use in a country with no commuting data at all?

Data and code

Commuting between the 98 Danish municipalities in 2024: where people live and where they work. Source: Statistics Denmark, table PEND100. Distances are between municipality centres.

  • flows.csv: origin, destination, number of commuters (also by sex)
  • municipalities.csv: workers living there, jobs, coordinates of the centre
  • distances.csv: distance in km for every pair
  • Notebook with all the code behind the playground: mobility_models.ipynb (or as a script: mobility_models.py)
